# Answer-key review: instructions for reviewers

This is the text sent to each reviewer with their packet.

---

## What the study is

The study tested how two AI configurations handle rule-governed assessment-support work in Philippine higher
education: reading a policy excerpt and answering a question about it, such as whether a special-exam
request is complete or how a modular grade average is computed. It used 24 written cases built on the public
rules of nine Philippine institutions, and compared answers released directly against answers passed through
automatic checks first. It is independent research; no institution commissioned it and no institution has
endorsed it.

## What we are asking you to judge

Each case has an **answer key**: a reference answer, and a numbered list of criteria a correct answer has to
meet, each pointing to a place in the quoted rule. The keys decided every correctness number in the study.

They were drafted with AI assistance and settled by one person. No independent expert checked them. That is
the study's biggest weakness, and you are being asked to fix it.

**You are judging the key, not the AI.** Your packet contains no AI answers, no model names and no scores,
and none will be added to it. For each criterion you say:

- Is this criterion correct as stated? Correct / Incorrect / Unclear / Out of scope
- Does the locator point to text that supports it? Yes / No / Cannot tell
- Any correction or concern, in your own words

Then, for the case as a whole: would a Philippine HEI registrar or faculty member accept this reference
answer as correct under the quoted rule? Yes / Yes with changes / No. There is also a box for concerns about
the rule version or how the rule would be read in practice.

Judge the key against the excerpt printed in the packet, because that excerpt is all the AI was given. If
you know the rule has since changed, or that the rule is applied differently in practice, that belongs in
the rule-version box rather than in the criterion judgment.

## How long it takes

About 15 to 25 minutes per case: reading the scenario, the excerpt and the key, then three to five criterion
judgments. A three-case packet is roughly an hour. This is an estimate from the length of the materials, not
a measured figure, and some cases with long excerpts take longer. There is no timer in the packet and no
per-item timing is collected.

## "Cannot tell" is a real answer

Use "Unclear" and "Cannot tell" rather than guessing. A criterion you cannot resolve is a finding about the
key, and it is recorded as one. Leaving a field blank is also fine; a blank is recorded as not answered.

Please work on your own. Do not compare notes with the other reviewer before returning your file, and please
do not ask an AI tool to judge the keys for you. Return what you have even if it is incomplete, and say
where you stopped. Partly filled packets are usable.

## How your input will be used

- Every judgment and comment you write is recorded as you wrote it. Nothing is edited or reworded.
- Your return is kept as you sent it, before any discussion.
- Every criterion you challenge is reported, whether or not the key is changed as a result.
- A key is changed only with a dated, published note naming the case, the criterion, your concern and the
  change. Nothing is edited quietly.
- Where you and the other reviewer disagree, both readings are reported. The researcher does not resolve the
  disagreement by choosing a side.
- Aggregate results and the disputed criteria are published. Your individual file is not.

## Credit

Reviewers are named in the acknowledgements **only** with recorded permission. There is a box on the cover
of the packet; answer Yes, No or Ask me later. If you say no, your contribution is reported without your
name and nothing identifiable is attributed to you. You can change your answer at any time before
publication.

## Data and ethics

No student data is involved. The scenarios are constructed for the study: the students, staff, courses and
grades in them are invented, and the proposed actions are test conditions, not descriptions of anyone's
conduct. The only real material is the public policy text quoted in each case, with its source, URL and hash
listed so you can check it yourself.

We collect your name or chosen label, your role and relevant experience, optionally your institution, the
date, and your judgments. Nothing else. You may withdraw your return at any time before publication and it
will be deleted.

This is a review of research materials, not an institutional review or an endorsement. Taking part does not
commit you or your institution to anything, and it does not mean you agree with the study's conclusions.

## Filling in the packet

1. Open the `.docx` in Microsoft Word and save your own copy.
2. The yellow fields are where you answer. Click a dropdown and pick an option, or click a text box and
   type.
3. If the dropdowns do not work in your app, type one of the listed options in the same place, spelled as
   listed.
4. Fill in the cover: your name or label, your role, the date, and your answer on acknowledgement.
5. Send the saved file back.

Questions at any point are welcome, including questions about a case you think is badly built. That is
useful information.
