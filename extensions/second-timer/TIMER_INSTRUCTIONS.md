# What you are doing today

Thank you for helping.

You will look at ten answers written by an AI assistant. Each one answers a question about a
university rule. For each answer, you decide what you would do with it, and then do it.

The computer records how long each step takes. That is the whole point of the exercise.

## You are not being tested

We are not measuring how good you are. We are measuring how long this kind of checking takes.

- There is no score.
- There is no right answer we are waiting for.
- Nobody will compare you with anyone else.
- Slow is fine. Fast is fine. Changing your mind is fine.

If you find an answer confusing, that is information about the answer, not about you. Say so in the
reason box.

## Where the material comes from

- The situations are made up for this study. They are not real cases.
- There is no student data of any kind. No names, no grades, no records.
- The rules quoted are public policy documents from Philippine universities.
- The answers were written by AI assistants some months ago. You will not be told which one.

## How each item works

**Step 1. One question before you see anything.**
The screen asks whether you have read this answer, or worked on this situation, before today. Answer
Yes or No honestly. Either answer is fine. We ask before showing you anything so the question is
not affected by what you see.

**Step 2. Clock 1 starts. Read and decide.**
You see the situation, the task, the piece of policy that governs it, and the AI answer. Read as
carefully as you normally would. When you have decided what to do with the answer, choose a decision
and write a short reason. One or two sentences is enough. The reason is required.

Your choices:

| Choice | Means |
|---|---|
| **accept** | Good as it is. You would use it without changing anything. |
| **minor edit** | Nearly right. A small fix: wording, a missing reference, a small clarification. |
| **major edit** | Substantially wrong or incomplete. You would rewrite a large part of it. |
| **reject** | You would not use it. |
| **escalate** | You would not decide this yourself. It goes to someone else, for example the registrar or a committee. |
| **accept gate** | Only for items where the computer has already held the answer back: you agree with holding it back. |

The line between **accept** and **minor edit** is simple: if you would change even one word before
using it, that is a minor edit, not accept.

**Step 3. Clock 1 stops. Clock 2 is for the work.**
If the answer needs no change at all, write one line saying you would use it as it stands, and press
the button that records no further work. Clock 2 stays at zero.

If it needs any change, press Start Clock 2 first, then do the work. Write the answer you would
actually use, or say what you would do instead. Then press Save.

Please do not start editing before Clock 2 is running. That is the one thing that spoils the
measurement.

## Some answers are hidden

For some items the computer's automatic check held the answer back. You will see the check result
but not the answer.

You may open it with the button on screen. Opening it is recorded. That is not a problem and it is
not a rule against opening it. We just need to know whether you did. You may also decide without
opening it, if the check result is enough for you.

## If something interrupts you

Press **Pause** and write one line about what happened. Press **Resume** when you come back.

When you resume, the screen asks whether the interruption started before you managed to press
Pause. Answer honestly. Say yes if you were pulled away and pressed Pause a bit later. It is a very
common thing and we would rather know.

If you step away without pausing, the screen will notice and ask what happened. Just write it down.
Nothing is lost and no time is guessed at.

If your computer restarts or the browser closes, reopen the page. Everything you have finished is
saved. The item you were on will be marked as interrupted, which is fine.

## How long it takes

About 30 to 45 minutes in total for all ten items. Some items are quick, a minute or two. Some take
longer.

You do not have to do all ten in one sitting. Use Pause, or tell the researcher, and come back.

## A few practical things

- Work as you normally would. Read at your normal speed.
- Do not look things up on the internet. Use the policy text on screen.
- Do not ask the researcher what the correct answer is. They will not tell you, on purpose.
- You may ask about buttons, the screen, or anything that does not work.
- If you want to stop at any point, stop. Say so and nothing more is asked of you.

## What happens to your answers

- Your times and decisions are saved in one file on this computer.
- Your name is not in that file, and your name is not published.
- Results are reported as "a second reviewer", beside the researcher's own times. The two sets are
  never mixed together.
- If you would like to be thanked by name in the paper, say so. If you would rather not, that is the
  default.

Thank you again.
